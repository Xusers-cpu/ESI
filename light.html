PHYSICS • CBSE CLASSES 9–10

Light & Optics

Study reflection, refraction, spherical mirrors, lenses and the human eye.

Learning objective

Use ray diagrams and the laws of reflection and refraction to explain how images form.

Reflection of light

Reflection occurs when light returns to the same medium after striking a surface. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection; both angles are measured from the normal.

A plane mirror forms a virtual, erect image of the same size as the object. The image appears as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

Spherical mirrors

A concave mirror curves inward and can form real or virtual images depending on object position. A convex mirror curves outward and forms a virtual, erect and diminished image for a real object.

Know the pole (P), principal axis, centre of curvature (C), principal focus (F) and focal length (f).

Refraction of light

Refraction is the change in direction of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium into another because its speed changes. Frequency remains constant across the boundary, while wavelength can change.

Light bends towards the normal when it enters a medium in which it travels more slowly, and away from the normal when it enters a medium in which it travels faster.

Lenses and image formation

A convex lens converges parallel rays and a concave lens diverges them. The nature and size of an image depend on the lens and the object distance.

Ray diagrams are a reliable way to determine whether an image is real or virtual, erect or inverted, and magnified or diminished.

The human eye

The eye lens focuses light onto the retina. The iris controls the size of the pupil, which regulates how much light enters. The ciliary muscles change the curvature of the lens for focusing.

Myopia (short-sightedness) is commonly corrected with a concave lens; hypermetropia (long-sightedness) is commonly corrected with a convex lens.

Key formulas

Mirror formula1/f = 1/v + 1/u
Magnification (mirror)m = −v/u
Lens formula1/f = 1/v − 1/u
Power of a lensP = 1/f (f in metres)

Use the sign convention taught in your CBSE textbook. Lens power is measured in dioptres (D).
