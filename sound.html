PHYSICS • CBSE CLASSES 9–10

Sound

Explore how vibrations produce sound and how sound waves travel through matter.

Learning objective

Connect vibrations with wave properties such as amplitude, frequency, time period and wavelength.

How sound is produced

Sound is produced by vibrating objects. These vibrations create disturbances that travel through a material medium as sound waves. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum because it needs particles to transmit the disturbance.

Wave properties

Amplitude relates to the size of the vibration and influences loudness. Frequency is the number of complete oscillations per second and is measured in hertz (Hz); it influences pitch. The time period is the time for one oscillation.

In air, sound travels as longitudinal waves made of compressions and rarefactions.

Speed of sound

Wave speed depends on the medium and its conditions. Sound generally travels faster in solids than in gases. In a given medium, the relationship between speed, frequency and wavelength is useful for solving numerical problems.

Echoes and reverberation

An echo is a reflected sound heard separately from the original sound when the delay is sufficient. Reverberation is the persistence of sound due to multiple reflections. Auditoriums use sound-absorbing materials to control excessive reverberation.

Hearing and ultrasound

The human ear detects a limited range of frequencies, often described approximately as 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz for a young healthy listener. Ultrasound has a frequency above this range and is used in applications such as medical imaging and detecting flaws in materials.

Key formulas

Wave speedv = fλ
Frequencyf = 1/T
Distanced = vt

Symbols: v = wave speed, f = frequency, λ = wavelength, T = time period.

✦Remember
  • Sound needs a material medium to propagate.
  • Higher frequency generally means higher pitch.
  • Amplitude and frequency describe different wave properties.
