Magnetism & Magnetic Effects

Understand magnetic fields, current-carrying conductors, electromagnets and electric motors.

Learning objective

Describe magnetic field patterns and explain how electric current can produce magnetic effects.

Magnetic fields

A magnetic field is the region in which a magnetic force can be experienced. Field lines outside a bar magnet are conventionally drawn from the north pole to the south pole. Field lines never cross, and closer lines represent a stronger field.

Current and magnetic fields

A current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field around it. The field pattern around a straight wire is circular. The direction can be found using the right-hand thumb rule: point the right thumb in the direction of conventional current; curled fingers show the field direction.

Solenoids and electromagnets

A solenoid is a long coil of wire. When current flows through it, its field resembles that of a bar magnet. An iron core can strengthen the field, producing an electromagnet that can be switched on and off by controlling current.

Force on a current-carrying conductor

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field can experience a force. The direction depends on the current and field directions. This effect is used in electric motors, which convert electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Electromagnetic induction

When the magnetic field linked with a conductor or coil changes, an induced voltage can be produced. If the circuit is closed, an induced current may flow. Generators use electromagnetic induction to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy.

Key formulas

Ohm’s lawV = IR
Electrical powerP = VI

✦Remember
  • Magnetic field lines outside a bar magnet go from north to south.
  • Current can create a magnetic field.
  • Motors and generators use related electromagnetic principles but convert energy in opposite directions.
