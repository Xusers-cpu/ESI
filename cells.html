Cells & Life

Discover the cell as the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.

Learning objective

Identify major cell parts and compare plant and animal cells.

What is a cell?

A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. All living organisms are made of one or more cells. Some organisms, such as Amoeba, consist of a single cell; plants and animals are multicellular.

Unicellular

One cell carries out all life processes.

Multicellular

Different cells specialise and work together.

Main parts of a cell

Cell membrane: surrounds the cell and regulates movement of substances in and out. Cytoplasm: jelly-like material where many chemical reactions occur. Nucleus: contains genetic material and helps control cell activities.

Plant cells also have a cellulose cell wall, a large central vacuole and plastids such as chloroplasts in photosynthetic cells. Mitochondria release usable energy during cellular respiration; ribosomes make proteins.

Movement across the membrane

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration. Osmosis is the movement of water through a selectively permeable membrane from a region of higher water concentration to lower water concentration.

Cells exchange gases, water and dissolved substances with their surroundings. The cell membrane helps regulate this exchange.

✦Remember

  • The nucleus contains genetic material in most eukaryotic cells.
  • Plant cell walls provide support; chloroplasts enable photosynthesis in suitable cells.
