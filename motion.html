PHYSICS • CBSE CLASSES 9–10

Motion & Forces

Review distance, displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration and Newton’s laws of motion.

Learning objective

Use motion quantities and Newton’s laws to describe and calculate how objects move.

Describing motion

Distance is the total path length, while displacement is the change in position with direction. Speed is distance travelled per unit time; velocity is displacement per unit time.

Motion graphs

The slope of a distance–time graph gives speed when the graph is a straight line. The slope of a velocity–time graph gives acceleration; the area under a velocity–time graph gives displacement for the interval.

Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It can occur when speed changes, direction changes, or both. SI units are m/s².

Newton’s laws

First law: an object remains at rest or in uniform straight-line motion unless acted on by a net external force. Second law: net force relates to mass and acceleration. Third law: forces between interacting objects occur in equal and opposite pairs acting on different objects.

Key formulas

Speedspeed = distance / time
Accelerationa = (v − u) / t
Newton’s second lawF = ma
Uniform accelerationv = u + at
s = ut + ½at²

Visual guide

The slope of a velocity–time graph represents acceleration.

✦Remember
  • Speed is scalar; velocity includes direction.
  • The slope of a velocity–time graph gives acceleration.
  • Net force is related to mass and acceleration.
