Electricity

Build confidence with electric current, potential difference, resistance and circuit calculations.

Learning objective

Explain the flow of charge in a circuit and apply the main Class 10 electricity relationships.

Electric charge and current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a cross-section of a conductor. A circuit needs a complete conducting path for a steady current to flow.

Conventional current is defined as the direction positive charge would move. In metal wires, electrons drift in the opposite direction.

Potential difference

Potential difference between two points is the work done per unit charge to move charge between them. A cell or battery provides energy to charges in a circuit.

Unit reminder

Current is measured in amperes (A), charge in coulombs (C), and potential difference in volts (V).

Resistance and Ohm’s law

Resistance describes how strongly a component opposes current. For an ohmic conductor at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to potential difference across it.

Resistance depends on the material, length, cross-sectional area and temperature of the conductor.

Series and parallel circuits

In a series circuit, the same current passes through each component. In a parallel circuit, each branch has the same potential difference, while current divides between branches.

Electric power and heating

Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred. A resistor can convert electrical energy into heat. The heating effect is used in devices such as electric heaters and toasters.

For household safety, avoid overloaded sockets and damaged wires. Never experiment with mains electricity; use only low-voltage educational circuits.
